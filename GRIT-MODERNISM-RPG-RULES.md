## 1. Introduction & Philosophy

Sit down. Get comfortable. Somebody go on and get the good chairs from the dining room — this gon' take a minute, but it's worth it.

Griot Modernism RPG is a new kind of tabletop system, built for **Concrete Kings: The Block Chronicles**. It ain't a reskin. It ain't D&D with a durag on. It's a system built from the ground up around one idea: **the block is the world, the cookout is the arena, and the cards in your hand tell the story better than any die roll ever could.**

In West African tradition, the Griot is the keeper of the oral history — the singer of songs, the rememberer of names, the one who carries the community's story forward so it's never lost. That's what your Game Master is here. Not a dungeon master trying to kill your character. Not a rules cop counting squares on a battle grid. A **Griot** — a storyteller who holds the block's memory and hands you the mic when it's your turn to add to it.

### 1.1 The Five Pillars

**Cards ARE the Rules.** In most RPGs, cards are a gimmick bolted onto dice. Here, the cards do the mechanical work. No dice. No twenty-stat character sheet. No two-hundred-page rulebook you gotta reference mid-scene. You play a card, the effect's right there, the story moves.

**Narrative Over Numbers.** The system rewards the best story, not the best-optimized build. A player who plays a weaker card with a stronger story beat should walk away feeling like they won something — because they did.

**Cultural Authenticity Over Generic Fantasy.** This ain't medieval Europe with the serial numbers filed off. Black urban life is the default setting. AAVE is the default voice. The barbershop, the cookout, the block, the church — these are the dungeons and the taverns, and they hit different because they're real.

**Accessibility Over Simulation.** A new player should understand the whole core loop in five minutes flat. A veteran player should still be finding new strategic depth twenty sessions in. Both of those things gotta be true at once, or the system failed.

**Legacy Over One-Shot.** This game is built to be lived in. Choices persist. Receipts stack up. Beefs simmer for months. Characters grow old on the block, and the block remembers everything they did.

### 1.2 What This System Replaces

We're not doing:

- ❌ D&D 5e with different card art
- ❌ Dice-based resolution
- ❌ Twenty stats and fifty skills
- ❌ Combat as the main event
- ❌ Medieval fantasy setting
- ❌ Alignment charts
- ❌ Vancian spell slots
- ❌ Grid-based tactical combat

We are doing:

- ✅ Card-driven resolution, full stop
- ✅ Four stats, max
- ✅ Narrative-first mechanics
- ✅ Cultural specificity, not a costume
- ✅ Urban setting as the default, not the "modern" reskin
- ✅ AAVE as the native voice of the rules, not a flavor filter
- ✅ Legacy and campaign play baked into the core loop
- ✅ A system that scales from casual party game to deep RPG campaign without changing engines

### 1.3 The Core Innovation — The Card As Mechanic

Every card in the Concrete Kings deck pulls double duty as art *and* rule. You don't need a side rulebook to know what "The DJ done played that song three times already" does at the table — the card carries its own weight.

- **Black Cards (Scenarios) = Challenges.** Presented by the Griot or drawn from the deck. Players respond with white cards. The Griot judges narrative fit and mechanical effect together.
- **White Cards (Responses) = Actions.** Every white card hides a mechanical effect until it's played — stat bonus, resource swing, narrative twist, relationship shift.
- **Receipt Cards = Consequences.** Persistent effects that carry from session to session. Your character's paper trail. Your legacy in physical form.
- **Cipher Cards = Rule Modifiers.** Temporary chaos, temporary strategy, temporary culture — each one changes the round in a clear, printed way.

That's the whole engine. Everything else in this book is what you build on top of it.

## 2. Character Creation (5 Minutes)

Character creation in Griot Modernism RPG takes five minutes because it's supposed to. You're not filling out a tax form. You're picking who you are on the block, and then you're playing.

## 3. Core Mechanics — Cards As Dice

### 3.1 No Dice. Cards Are Your Dice.

When a character needs to resolve an action, the Griot presents a **Black Card** — a challenge. The player responds with a **White Card** — an action. The Griot judges the response on four weighted factors:

| Factor | Weight | What It's Asking |
|---|---:|---|
| Narrative Fit | 40% | Does the response make sense in the story? |
| Mechanical Effect | 30% | What does the card actually do? |
| Cultural Authenticity | 20% | Does it feel real? |
| Entertainment Value | 10% | Is it funny, clever, or moving? |

**Resolution Scale (0–100 total):**

| Score | Result |
|---|---|
| 60+ | Success with benefit |
| 40–59 | Partial success |
| 20–39 | Failure with opportunity |
| 0–19 | Full failure, new problem |

The Griot doesn't need to show their math out loud every time — a good Griot develops a feel for the scale fast. The table above is there for new Griots and for settling disputes, not for slowing down every single beat.

**Why no dice?** Dice put distance between the player and the story. A die says "you got a 17." A card says "you played *The DJ done played that song three times* and the whole block nodded." One of those is a number. The other one is a moment everybody at the table remembers next week.

### 3.2 The Card As Action

Every white card carries a hidden mechanical effect, revealed the moment it's played.

**Stat Bonus** — `+2 SC this round` · `+1 COM for 3 rounds` · `+3 WIS at Church` · `+1 REP per win`

**Narrative Twist** — *the situation changes* (the police show up) · *an ally appears* (your cousin has your back) · *a secret revealed* (you learn something about the NPC)

**Resource Gain** — `+$100 cash` · `+2 items` · `+1 Receipt`

**Relationship Effect** — `+1 alliance with [player/NPC]` · `-1 beef with [player/NPC]` · `new beef with [NPC]`

**Cipher Modifier** — `next Cipher is [specific cipher]` · `Cipher effect is doubled` · `Cipher is canceled`

**Special** — `auto-success this round` · `re-roll any card` · `draw 3 extra cards`

Effects are printed on the card but stay face-down in play until the moment it's revealed — that's where the tension lives. The Griot keeps a master effect list on hand for reference so nobody's arguing about what a card does mid-scene.

## 4. The Four Stats

Four stats. That's the whole sheet. Anybody tells you they need a fifth, they're overthinking it.

**Street Cred (SC)** — How much the block respects you. Used for intimidation, hustles, street negotiations. High SC: people listen, doors open, beefs get avoided before they start. Low SC: you get clowned, skipped over, tested by everybody with something to prove.

**Community (COM)** — How embedded you are in the block. Used for alliances, events, collective action. High COM: alliances hold, events unlock, help shows up when you need it. Low COM: you're isolated, no backup, doors stay closed.

**Wisdom (WIS)** — What you've actually learned from the block. Used for Cipher control, card draws, foresight. High WIS: better cards, better choices, you see it coming. Low WIS: bad draws, poor choices, blindsided every time.

**Reputation (REP)** — Your story's weight in the neighborhood. Used for O.G. powers, media attention, legacy. High REP: people know your name, doors open before you knock, your legacy grows on its own. Low REP: you're a mystery nobody trusts yet, or worse, forgotten.

## 5. The Griot Role

### 5.1 The Griot Is

- The storyteller
- The rule arbiter
- The voice of the block
- The keeper of the world's memory

### 5.2 The Griot Is NOT

- An adversary
- A dungeon master trying to kill the players
- A rigid rules enforcer
- A narrator who controls everything

### 5.3 Griot Powers

Present challenges (black cards) · Judge responses (white cards) · Describe outcomes · Control NPCs · Trigger events · Award Receipts · Track legacy.

### 5.4 Griot Limitations

Cannot force players into specific choices · cannot override a printed card effect · cannot change established facts · must respect player agency · must keep the story moving.

### 5.5 Griot Preparation Checklist

Before every session, a Griot should have:

- 20+ Black Cards ready
- 5–10 NPCs with real personalities
- 3–5 locations with real detail
- 2–3 potential events
- 1–2 Receipt chains loaded and ready to fire
- One clear session goal

## 6. Conflict Resolution

### 6.1 No Traditional Combat

There's no hit points here. No initiative order. No armor class. Conflict in Griot Modernism RPG is about narrative stakes, cultural specificity, and choices that mean something — not tracking damage on a sheet.

**Four Conflict Types:**

**1. Verbal Conflict (The Dozens)** — Players trade white cards back and forth. The Griot (or a neutral third party) decides who won. Winner gains +2 SC. Loser loses −1 COM. Can escalate into physical conflict if it gets out of hand.

**2. Physical Conflict** — A series of card plays, best 2 of 3. Each card played reveals a move and its effect; the Griot narrates the outcome. Nobody dies. Consequences are narrative — injured, arrested, humiliated, whatever fits the story.

**3. Systemic Conflict** — Receipt-based, multi-round, aimed at institutions: police, landlords, school boards. Requires alliances, strategy, and real community support. Winning changes the block permanently.

**4. Internal Conflict** — Receipt-driven personal dilemmas. No cards needed — pure roleplay. The Griot presents a scenario, the player chooses, and the consequences hit stats permanently.

### 6.2 The Beat System

Every conflict breaks into **Beats**. One card play = one Beat.

**3-Beat Conflict (Quick):** Beat 1 — Challenge presented · Beat 2 — Response played · Beat 3 — Outcome determined.

**5-Beat Conflict (Standard):** Beat 1 — Setup · Beat 2 — Escalation · Beat 3 — Climax · Beat 4 — Aftermath · Beat 5 — Resolution.

**7-Beat Conflict (Epic):** Beats 1–2 — Setup · Beats 3–4 — Escalation · Beat 5 — Climax · Beat 6 — Aftermath · Beat 7 — New normal.

**Beat Structure (every Beat, every time):**

1. Griot describes the situation
2. Player chooses a white card
3. Card effect is revealed
4. Griot narrates the outcome
5. Stats update
6. Move to next Beat, or resolve

## 7. The Beef System

### 7.1 What Is a Beef?

A Beef is a persistent conflict between two characters — not just "I don't like you," but a real story thread that drives the narrative forward until somebody deals with it.

### 7.2 Creating a Beef

Player A plays a card that negatively affects Player B. Player B can accept it or escalate. If it escalates, a Beef is created, and both players draw a **Beef Receipt**.

**Beef Receipt structure:**
- **Title:** "The [Event] Beef"
- **Description:** What actually happened
- **Choices:** *Squash it* (+1 COM) or *Stand on it* (+2 SC)
- **Persists** until resolved

### 7.3 Beef Effects

- −1 to all joint actions between the two players
- Cannot form an alliance while the Beef is active
- The Griot may introduce complications tied to it
- Other players can step in and mediate

### 7.4 Resolving a Beef

Either player can offer resolution; both must agree. A resolved Beef becomes "The Time We..." — a story, not just a stat line. Resolving it earns +1 COM for both, and +1 REP each if it was resolved publicly.

## 8. Campaign & Legacy

### 8.1 Campaign Structure

**Session Zero (2 hours):** character creation, world introduction, relationship building, campaign goal setting.

**Standard Session (3–4 hours):**
- Opening — 15 min: recaps, news, events
- Act 1 — 60 min: main objectives
- Act 2 — 60 min: complications, twists
- Act 3 — 45 min: climax, resolution
- Epilogue — 30 min: Receipts, legacy, next-session setup

**Campaign Length:**
- Short: 4–6 sessions (1–2 months)
- Standard: 12–16 sessions (3–4 months)
- Epic: 24–32 sessions (6–8 months)

### 8.2 Legacy Mechanics — What Persists Between Sessions

**Character:** stats (as modified by play) · resolved Receipts (full history) · items collected · relationships formed · reputation score · level/rank.

**World:** locations unlocked · NPCs met and remembered · events that occurred · physical changes to the block · political changes.

**Campaign:** active story threads · unresolved conflicts · upcoming events · season/weather · time progression.

### 8.3 The Block as Character

The block itself has stats, and it changes as the campaign goes:

| Block Stat | What It Tracks |
|---|---|
| Community | How tight-knit the neighborhood is |
| Safety | How safe the streets are |
| Vibrancy | How much life is on the block |
| Legacy | How much history the block holds |

Block stats move based on player actions, events, Receipt resolutions, alliances, and beefs.

**Block States:** Thriving (all stats high, events positive) · Struggling (some stats low, frequent challenges) · Gentrifying (COM down, SC up, REP mixed) · Forgotten (all stats low, events rare) · Legendary (all stats max, special endings unlock).

### 8.4 The Hustle Economy

Hustles generate income when their signature move lands:

| Hustle | Income |
|---|---|
| Street Hustler | +$50 per successful play |
| Community Organizer | +$100 per event organized |
| Creative | +$75 per creative play |
| Entrepreneur | +$100 per business deal |
| Student | +$25 per study session |
| Elder | +$75 per wisdom shared |
| Diplomat | +$50 per deal made |
| Rebel | +$30 per disruption caused |

**Income uses:** buy items, pay fines, fund events, bribe NPCs, invest in the block.

## 9. Receipt Chains

### 9.1 What Is a Receipt Chain?

A series of connected Receipts that tell a longer story. Complete the chain, get bonus points and unlock new content.

### 9.2 Example Chain — "The Cookout Saga"

**Receipt 1: "The Invite"** — You was the last one to get the invite. *Show up and show out* (+1 COM) or *skip it* (+1 SC). → Receipt 2 or 3.

**Receipt 2: "The Arrival"** — You showed up late and the plate was almost empty. *Make a plate anyway* (+1 SC) or *help in the kitchen* (+1 COM). → Receipt 4 or 5.

**Receipt 3: "The Absence"** — You skipped it and Uncle Leroy noticed. *Call and explain* (+1 COM) or *let it ride* (+1 SC). → Receipt 6.

**Receipt 4: "The Potato Salad Incident"** — Your potato salad started a family war. *Share the recipe* (+2 COM) or *keep it secret* (+2 REP). → Receipt 7.

**Receipt 5: "The Grill Master Beef"** — You tried to help with the grill and offended the master. *Apologize* (+1 COM) or *stand on it* (+2 SC). → Receipt 8.

**Receipt 6: "The Call Back"** — Uncle Leroy called you out. *Explain yourself* (+1 COM) or *clap back* (+2 SC). → Receipt 9.

**Receipt 7: "The Resolution"** — The family war ended with you mediating. *Take credit* (+1 REP) or *give credit* (+2 COM). **Chain Complete:** +5 SC, +5 COM, Special Achievement.

### 9.3 Chain Benefits

Completing a chain gives permanent stat bonuses, unlocks special cards, unlocks special Origins, unlocks special locations, and creates a legacy story the block remembers.

### 9.4 The Receipt Economy

Receipts aren't just flavor — they're currency. They can be traded between players, used as bargaining chips, collected for power, or shared to build COM.

**Rarity:** Common 60% · Uncommon 25% · Rare 10% · Legendary 5%.

**Market rules:** players can trade Receipts freely · the Griot can award Receipts as rewards · Receipts can be stolen (via a Beef) · Receipts can be destroyed (via O.G. power).

## 11. Advanced Rules — Ciphers, Events, Alliances

### 11.1 The Griot's Toolbox

**Flashback** — pause the current scene, show a past moment that informs the present. Use Receipts as flashback triggers. 5–10 minutes.

**Time Skip** — jump forward days, weeks, months. Show how choices affected the world. Update stats, relationships, block state. 2–5 minutes.

**Parallel Scene** — show what other characters are doing at the same time. Multiple players can run simultaneous scenes; the Griot rotates between them. 10–15 minutes per scene.

**The Block Remembers** — reference past Receipts, choices, and consequences constantly. Make the world feel persistent. NPCs should reference a player's reputation. Old beefs, old alliances, old wins all still matter.

### 11.2 The Cipher System (RPG Mode)

Each Cipher changes one rule for one round. The Griot announces it at round start; everybody adapts.

| Cipher | Effect |
|---|---|
| ☮ Peace | No conflict this round. All players gain +1 COM. |
| 🔥 Barbershop Truth | All cards played anonymously. Griot judges blind. |
| 💰 Block Watch | All players re-play from hand. Chaos. |
| 📻 Side Door | Lowest-REP player gets one free submission from the discard pile. |
| 🎤 Mic Drop | Round winner draws one extra Receipt. |
| 👑 Crown | The O.G. (highest REP) wins automatically. |
| 🍖 Potluck | Everyone contributes one card to a shared pool. Griot picks from the pool. |
| 🔁 Rewind | Undo the last Receipt. The story changes. |
| ⚡ Flash | All players reveal one card from hand. |
| 🎯 Target | The O.G. calls out one player, who must play an extra card. |
| 🤝 Handshake | The two lowest-COM players form an automatic alliance. |
| 🔥 Fire | The highest-SC player chooses one stat to boost by +2. |

### 11.3 The Event System (RPG Mode)

Events are major world occurrences — triggered by the Griot or by card effects — that hit every player at once.

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| Block Party | +1 COM for all, music, dancing, food |
| Police Raid | −1 SC for all nearby, must hide or run |
| Fire | Location closed for 2 rounds, +2 COM for anyone who helps |
| Parade | +1 REP for participants |
| Protest | +2 COM if you join, −1 REP if you ignore it |
| Church Service | +1 COM, +1 REP for attending |
| Cookout | +1 COM, +1 REP for attending |
| Gentrification Protest | +2 COM if you join, −1 SC if you cross the line |
| Funeral | +1 COM for attending, −1 REP for disrespect |
| Celebration | +1 REP for attending |

**Resolution:** the Griot describes the event, players choose how to respond, card plays resolve individual actions, and the collective outcome gets determined together.

### 11.4 The Alliance System (RPG Mode)

**Forming:** two or more players agree to work together. Each needs COM +2. Shared resources, cards, information, and combined stat bonuses follow.

**Alliance Powers:** shared hand size (+2 cards) · shared Receipts (both get the benefit) · shared Cipher control (can coordinate) · shared victory (if one wins, both get +1).

**Breaking:** either player can break it at any time. Breaking costs −2 COM for both. Doing it publicly creates a Beef, and may trigger a Receipt.

## 12. Sample Adventure & Combat Encounter

### 12.1 "The Cookout Crisis"

**Session Length:** 3–4 hours · **Players:** 3–6 · **Level:** 1–5 · **Setting:** The Block, summer evening

**Act 1 — The Invitation (30 min)**

> *Griot:* "It's Saturday. The block is buzzing. You hear the music from three blocks away. The smell of charcoal and fried chicken cuts through the summer air. Your phone buzzes — group chat: 'Cookout at Marcus's tonight. Bring something.'"

**Challenge Card:** *"The DJ done played that song 3 times already"*

- Marcus (Street Hustler) plays *"The same 5 songs on repeat"* → +2 SC, the block nods.
- Aisha (Community Organizer) plays *"The plate line was 30 minutes long"* → +1 COM, she organized the line.
- DeShawn (Creative) plays *"Somebody took over the aux and played the whole Lil Wayne album"* → +1 REP, the crowd goes crazy.

**Outcome:** The cookout is lit. +1 COM for all.

**Act 2 — The Crisis (60 min)**

**Challenge Card:** *"The police showed up at the party because..."*

- Marcus plays *"Somebody called them on the basketball game"* → −1 SC, but now he knows who called.
- Aisha plays *"The music was too loud"* → she negotiates with the officer, +1 COM.
- DeShawn plays *"They thought it was a fight but it was just the dance"* → +2 REP, he de-escalates.

**Beef Created:** Marcus knows who called. Receipt: *"The Snitch Beef."*

**Griot Twist:** The police leave, but the mood shifted. The cookout ain't the same.

**Act 3 — The Resolution (45 min)**

**Challenge Card:** *"You got to the cookout and realized..."*

- Marcus plays *"The mac and cheese was already gone"* → −1 SC, he missed the plate.
- Aisha plays *"Auntie made you a to-go plate"* → +2 COM, the block takes care of its own.
- DeShawn plays *"The DJ played your song"* → +1 REP, a moment of pure joy.

**Outcome:** The cookout ended, but the block grew closer. Alliances formed. Beefs noted. Legacy written.

**Epilogue (30 min)**

**Receipts Distributed:** "The Cookout Crisis" (resolved) · "The Snitch Beef" (unresolved — carries to next session) · "The Mac and Cheese Sacrifice" (resolved, +1 REP)

**Legacy Update:** The Block — COM +1 · Marcus — SC −1, gained loyalty · Aisha — COM +2, reputation as peacemaker · DeShawn — REP +2, reputation as entertainer.

**Next Session Hook:** *"Sunday morning. The church parking lot is full. Deacon Williams approaches you. 'We need to talk about what happened at the cookout.'"*

### 12.2 "The Barbershop Beef" — Sample Combat Encounter

**Setup:** Location — Barbershop · NPC — a barber who dissed your cut · Stakes — your reputation on the block.

**Beat 1 — The Diss**
> *Griot:* "You walk in. The barber looks at your hair and says..."

**Black Card:** *"The barber said your hairline was..."*
Player plays *"So high it had its own zip code"* → +2 SC, crowd laughs. Barber claps back with *"At least mine be growing"* → −1 SC.

**Beat 2 — The Escalation**
> *Griot:* "The barber's clientele is watching. This getting tense."

**Black Card:** *"The barbershop TV was stuck on..."*
Player plays *"The news and the barber arguing about politics"* → +1 REP, media on your side. Barber counters with *"The old-school album everybody know the words to"* → the crowd sides with the barber.

**Beat 3 — The Climax**
> *Griot:* "The chair you been sitting in for 2 hours is getting uncomfortable. The whole shop watching. You gotta say something."

**Black Card:** *"You walked into the barbershop and heard..."*
Player plays *"The barber saying your fade was 'interesting'"* → +2 SC, you clap back. Barber concedes, offers a free cut.

**Beat 4 — The Aftermath**
> *Griot:* "The barber gives you the cut. It's clean. The crowd nods. But you notice something..."

**Receipt:** *"The Barber's Respect"* — earned, but it cost you. Choices: accept the free cut (+1 COM) or pay full price (+2 REP).

**Beat 5 — The Resolution**
Player pays full price. **Outcome:** +2 REP, +1 SC, the barber respects you, Receipt resolved. **New Relationship:** Barber becomes an ally.

1. Review player characters and stats
2. Prepare 20+ Black Cards
3. Prepare 3–5 NPCs with personalities
4. Prepare 2–3 locations with detail

## 14. Variant Rules

### 14.1 Solo Play

One player controls one character, with 3 AI-controlled NPCs as allies/rivals and a Griot deck of 30 Black Cards. Core mechanics unchanged; Receipts persist, campaign continues.

**Special rules:** Ciphers drawn from a separate deck. Receipts drawn from a separate deck. The Griot keeps a "Fate" deck for twists.

### 14.2 Two-Player Play

Both players are protagonists. Griot deck handles challenges. No judge — both play cards, the Griot adjudicates. Alliance system active. Competitive or cooperative, your call.

**Special rules:** each player controls 2 characters — or 1 character each with Griot-provided NPC allies — or 1 character each, head-to-head.

### 14.3 Competitive Play

3–6 players. Last player standing wins. Alliances allowed but breakable. Beefs carry mechanical penalties. Winner takes all Reputation.

### 14.4 Cooperative Play

All players on the same team. The Griot presents challenges; players work together to overcome them. Shared victory or shared defeat. Focus stays on community building.

### 14.5 Campaign Play

Multi-session. Legacy persists. Receipt chains span sessions. Character growth is permanent. World changes are permanent. Epilogue runs between every session.

## 15. The O.G. System

### 15.1 What Is an O.G.?

A character who's reached max Reputation — unlocked every power, completed every Receipt chain, earned the block's ultimate respect.

### 15.2 O.G. Abilities

- Veto any Griot decision once per session
- Call a Block Meeting (all players must attend)
- Grant Blessings (+1 to any stat)
- Create new Receipts
- Modify Cipher effects
- Change the weather, narratively speaking

### 15.3 O.G. Responsibilities

Must attend Church · must attend Cookouts · must mentor younger players · must resolve conflicts · must tell the story of the block.

Power without responsibility ain't O.G. status — it's just being loud.

## 20. Success Criteria

The Griot Modernism RPG is ready when it hits every one of these, no exceptions:

1. **Learnable** — new players understand the core loop in five minutes
2. **Playable** — a full session runs 3–4 hours without confusion
3. **Deep** — strategic choices matter, multiple viable paths exist
4. **Cultural** — AAVE voice throughout, culturally specific content, not a costume
5. **Legacy** — choices persist between sessions in ways that actually matter
6. **Fun** — players laugh, argue, tell stories, and want to come back
7. **Accessible** — no dice, no complex math, no 200-page rulebook to reference mid-scene
8. **Scalable** — works for 2–6 players, solo, online, in-person
9. **Replayable** — 700+ cards, 100+ Receipts, 8 Origins, 8 Hustles
10. **Expandable** — new cards, new locations, new campaigns can be added without breaking the engine

### Anti-Patterns — What We Will Never Add

❌ Dice-based resolution · ❌ 20+ stats · ❌ Complex skill trees · ❌ Combat as the primary focus · ❌ Generic fantasy setting · ❌ Alignment system · ❌ Vancian magic · ❌ Grid-based combat · ❌ 200+ page rulebook · ❌ Rule-lawyer heaven · ❌ Cultural appropriation · ❌ Stereotypes · ❌ Pay-to-win mechanics · ❌ Grinding for power · ❌ Solo-only play · ❌ No legacy persistence
