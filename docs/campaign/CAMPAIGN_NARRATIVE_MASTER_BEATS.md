# Expanded First Miles Beats
## 13-beat structure with deterministic forks, rooted in real Black/urban culture

### Real-World Inspiration
- Setting: 125th Street corridor, Harlem — real Black cultural corridor since early 1900s
- Institutions: barbershop as sanctuary/economic engine, bodega as Puerto Rican/Dominican legacy community center
- Music: era-specific soundscapes — 1980s boomboxes, 1990s R&B/hip hop, 2000s crunk/snap, present-day drill/streaming
- Language: AAVE decade evolution — 1980s diss/rock, 1990s cap/no cap roots, 2000s finna/gon, present-day deadass/tea
- Comedy: Pryor-style raw storytelling, Martin sitcom timing, Chappelle social commentary, TikTok roast culture

### Beat 1 — Arrival
- Origin-specific opening grounded in real neighborhood economics
- Barber: Ray calls you back after mom's funeral; shop debt is personal; chair 7 still has your name on it
- Hustler: Marquez picks you up from holding; you owe him from 2023; precinct parking lot deal
- Mechanic: Your ride seized on 125th; Jenkins offers cash job; impound smell still in your jacket
- Student: Expelled; Dean says leave town; guidance counselor was in your corner quietly
- Fork: origin secret revealed or deferred
- Gates: none
- Outcomes: trust seed, heat seed, cash seed, first receipt opportunity

### Beat 2 — First Block
- Hub introduction
- Fork: who you approach first
- Gates: none
- Outcomes: first trust deltas, first flag opportunities

### Beat 3 — The Gossip (FIRST CARD PLAY)
- Jada at the bar needs a rumor checked; barback swears he saw Ray with a cop
- 1980s reference: Jada's bar plays Too $hort, VHS tapes behind the bar
- 1990s reference:jukebox loaded with Mary J. Blige, Biggie, method man
- 2000s reference: first iPhone photos on the bar, early Instagram
- Present-day reference: TikTok rumors, Cash App receipts, block Twitter
- Card: "Who's been skimming from the till?"
- Options: A) Confront Ray directly, B) Ask around quietly, C) Lie to Jada for cash
- Humor: barback tries to sell you a rumor; if you call him out, he offers free Henny
- Chaos: Jada's ex walks in; wrong answer makes the whole bar watch
- Mature: rumor involves a secret affair, money laundering, or both
- Consequences: trust+/-, heat+/-, secret flag trigger, possible receipt

### Beat 4 — Working Stiff
- Mini-game: Haircut Challenge
- Fork: speed vs precision
- Outcomes: cash, rep, Ray trust

### Beat 5 — The Missing Kid
- The Kid's brother hasn't come home; mom is on the block screaming his name
- 1990s reference: Brother was last seen near the bodega playing Game Boy
- 2000s reference: Last ping was near the park; his Sidekick phone is dead
- Present-day reference: His AirPods are dead; last TikTok was from the fire escape
- Humor: The Kid tries to act tough but is clearly scared; offers you his whole candy bag
- Chaos: If you bring the wrong cousin, they start fighting in the street
- Branch: Found sober / Found high / Already gone
- Mature: brother is hiding from a drug debt, not lost; may owe Marquez directly
- Outcomes: Kid trust, secret flag, heat delta, possible violence

### Beat 6 — Marquez Check-In
- Marquez wants a favor: look the other way during a pickup; says it's just a package
- 1980s reference: Marquez drives a Cutlass Supreme; baggie passed in the parking lot
- 1990s reference: Marquez has a brick phone; deal goes down near the payphone
- 2000s reference: Marquez has a Sidekick; deal is coordinated through AOL chat
- Present-day reference: Marquez has a burner iPhone; deal tracked through encrypted app
- Branch: accept / warn / refuse
- Humor: Marquez offers you $50 and a pizza; you both know the pizza is better than the cash
- Chaos: The target is someone's cousin; wrong choice starts a block beef
- Mature: the package is not drugs; it's a hard drive with evidence that could take down half the precinct
- Consequences: Marquez trust, heat, secret flag, possible betrayal setup

### Beat 7 — Night Falls
- Heat rises after dark unless player chose a safe path
- 1980s reference: sirens are closer; someone is running a portable TV on the stoop
- 1990s reference: car alarms go off; someone is playing Too $hort too loud
- 2000s reference: streetlights flicker; someone is filming a fight on a flip phone
- Present-day reference: drones buzz overhead; someone is live-streaming the raid
- Fork: low heat safe path / high heat confrontation
- Humor: if heat is low, Jada invites you to her back room; if heat is high, Marquez confronts you in the alley
- Chaos: a random fight breaks out; if you intervene, you gain trust but heat; if you run, you lose respect
- Mature: street encounter could be a mugging, a police stop, or a domestic dispute
- Outcomes: Jada or Marquez scene variant, heat delta, trust delta, possible secret flag

### Beat 8 — The Receipt
- First receipt acquisition; item ties to origin secret or missing-kid thread
- 1980s reference: receipt is a Polaroid photo of the old barbershop crew
- 1990s reference: receipt is a payphone card with a number written in marker
- 2000s reference: receipt is a Sidekick screenshot of a deleted text thread
- Present-day reference: receipt is a screenshot of a deleted Snap, Venmo request, or Cash App note
- Humor: the receipt is something embarrassing — your mom’s handwritten note, a bad check
- Chaos: the receipt is discovered by the wrong person; now they know your business
- Mature: receipt could be a paternity notice, an eviction warning, or a threat
- Outcomes: lore fragment, secret flag, possible trust shift with NPC who sees it

### Beat 9 — Act 1 Climax: The Raid
- Police raid the barbershop or bodega; lights flash; someone screams
- 1980s reference: police use bullhorns; everyone scatter like it's a drug bust
- 1990s reference: rap song cuts off mid-verse; someone yells "5-0"
- 2000s reference: cameras come out; someone is live-streaming the raid
- Present-day reference: air horn alerts on group chat; everybody knows before the cops arrive
- Branch: hide / run / talk
- Origin modifiers:
  - Barber: Ray takes the fall if player has high trust; "I got this, go"
  - Hustler: Marquez helps player escape if owed favor; "I got you, follow me"
  - Mechanic: Player can hotwire escape car; "I know where the keys are"
  - Student: Player can use knowledge of back exits; "I mapped this building in 10th grade"
- Humor: Jada grabs the bar's cashbox; Kid tries to hide behind the popcorn machine
- Chaos: The raid hits the wrong spot; you have to choose between saving evidence or saving a person
- Mature: violence is implied not shown; someone gets cuffed; someone gets hurt
- Outcomes: major trust shifts, heat spike, quest flags, possible arrest path

### Beat 10 — Cleanup
- Player must reduce heat or pay later; block is watching
- 1980s reference: old head on the stoop says "you bringing too much attention"
- 1990s reference: Jada says the bar is losing money because people are scared
- 2000s reference: Ray says his clippers are getting fewer customers; Instagram rumors spread fast
- Present-day reference: block Twitter account posts your photo; Cash App donations are down
- Branch: lay low at safe house / bribe Marquez / do Jada's errand
- Humor: safe house is Jenkins' couch; he charges you $20 for a blanket and old Cheetos
- Chaos: while you're laying low, a fire breaks out in the next building; everybody evacuates
- Mature: bribe Marquez means doing something worse than the original favor; Jada's errand is personal and risky
- Outcomes: heat delta, cash cost, trust delta, possible new secret flag

### Beat 11 — Old Man Jenkins
- Jenkins reveals he knew player's origin secret all along; says "I was there"
- 1980s reference: Jenkins shows you a VHS tape of the incident; his VCR still works
- 1990s reference: Jenkins has a filing cabinet full of Polaroids and handwritten notes
- 2000s reference: Jenkins has a burned CD with audio recordings; says "I don't trust the cloud"
- Present-day reference: Jenkins has an old iPhone with the original text thread; "I saved everything"
- Fork: trade secret / keep secret / refuse
- Humor: Jenkins offers you a peppermint and a lecture; the peppermint is from 1999
- Chaos: Jenkins' niece shows up unexpectedly; she knows more than Jenkins does
- Mature: Jenkins' secret could destroy a family or save the block; using it has a moral cost
- Outcomes: Jenkins trust, secret flag, map unlock, possible relationship shift

### Beat 12 — Chen's Problem
- Chen's shipments are being intercepted; he offers you a cut to investigate
- 1980s reference: shipments moved through payphones and duffel bags at bus stops
- 1990s reference: shipments moved through beepers and coded radio calls
- 2000s reference: shipments moved through Sidekick texts and Meet-Me spots
- Present-day reference: shipments tracked through encrypted apps and QR codes on bodega receipts
- Branch: work for Chen / work against Chen / play both sides
- Humor: Chen tries to offer you expired snacks as payment; Jada roasts him for it
- Chaos: the shipment is actually something completely different than you expected
- Mature: Chen's operation includes human trafficking, not just goods; wrong choice enables it
- Outcomes: Chen trust, cash, secret flag, possible heat spike

### Beat 13 — The Gossip Network
- Mini-game: connect nodes to reveal who controls the block
- 1980s reference: gossip traveled through payphones, barbershops, and church pews
- 1990s reference: gossip traveled through beepers, block parties, and bootleg CDs
- 2000s reference: gossip traveled through MySpace comments, text chains, and MySpace bulletins
- Present-day reference: gossip traveled through group chats, TikTok, Instagram close friends
- Fork: full connect / partial / fail
- Humor: one node is a random guy who just wants to sell you bootleg DVDs
- Chaos: wrong connection blames an innocent NPC; you have to fix it or live with it
- Mature: the real controller is someone you trust; uncovering it changes the whole game
- Outcomes: suspect list, heat delta, secret flag, possible relationship collapse

### Beat 14 — Ray or Marquez?
- Depending on prior choices, one NPC becomes the primary contact
- 1980s reference: Ray was the young boxer; Marquez was the rookie cop
- 1990s reference: Ray was running the shop; Marquez was on the take
- 2000s reference: Ray was trying to go legit; Marquez was running the block
- Present-day reference: Ray is trying to gentrify his own shop; Marquez is on the verge of a promotion
- Fork: Ray path / Marquez path / neutral
- Humor: both men deny everything; Jada laughs at you for believing either of them
- Chaos: choosing one locks the other into enemy status; they both show up at the same time
- Mature: the truth is both are compromised; you have to work with compromised people
- Outcomes: path lock, trust caps adjust, possible betrayal setup

### Beat 15 — Safe House Raid
- Player's safe house is hit; receipts scattered; someone sold you out
- 1980s reference: safe house is a basement apartment with a Zenith TV and a VCR
- 1990s reference: safe house has a cordless phone, a pager, and a stack of VHS tapes
- 2000s reference: safe house has a desktop PC, a router, and a iPod dock
- Present-day reference: safe house has smart speakers that get hacked; Nest cam catches the raid
- Fork: pursue / hide / burn evidence
- Humor: Jenkins' cat escapes with a receipt in its mouth; now you have to chase the cat
- Chaos: the raid is livestreamed; your reputation changes depending on how you react
- Mature: the betrayer is someone you invited in; trust is violated in the most intimate way
- Outcomes: receipts lost/found, heat spike, trust collapse, possible secret flag

### Beat 16 — The Betrayal
- One trusted NPC betrays player based on earlier choice; reveal is public
- 1980s reference: betrayal happens at the barbershop; everyone hears the argument
- 1990s reference: betrayal happens at the cookout; someone records it on a camcorder
- 2000s reference: betrayal happens over a text thread that gets forwarded to everyone
- Present-day reference: betrayal happens in a group chat that gets screenshotted and posted
- Fork: pay ransom / storm location / leverage secret
- Humor: The Kid tries to negotiate with a candy bar; it almost works
- Chaos: two NPCs betray you at once; you have to choose who to save
- Mature: betrayal is over money, love, or survival; no one is purely evil
- Outcomes: Kid state, trust collapse, Act 3 flag, possible death path

### Beat 17 — Preparation
- Final side activity before climax; you can only do one
- 1980s reference: Ray offers you a free cut and a polo shirt; Jada gives you a pager number
- 1990s reference: Ray gives you a fade and a CD; Jada gives you a beeper code
- 2000s reference: Ray gives you a haircut and an iPod shuffle; Jada gives you a Sidekick app
- Present-day reference: Ray gives you a cut and a Spotify playlist; Jada gives you a Cash App loan
- Fork: Ray / Jada / Chen / Jenkins
- Humor: each option has a ridiculous catch; Ray's cut takes 3 hours; Chen's item is expired
- Chaos: while you're preparing, something else goes wrong; you have to improvise
- Mature: each preparation has a moral cost; nothing is free
- Outcomes: rep, cash, modifier, secret item, possible relationship change

### Beat 18 — The Plan
- Route selection: legal / street / violent / secret
- 1980s reference: legal route uses press and church; street route uses barbershop network; violent route uses weapons; secret route uses old files
- 1990s reference: legal route uses newspaper and lawyer; street route uses block vote; violent route uses fists and pipes; secret route uses evidence tapes
- 2000s reference: legal route uses blogs and email leaks; street route uses MySpace and text blasts; violent route uses guns; secret route uses hacked emails
- Present-day reference: legal route uses Twitter and TikTok; street route uses group chats; violent route uses drones; secret route uses leaked texts
- Locked options by flags/trusts/heat
- Humor: the plan is assembled from card choices; it's inevitably ridiculous but somehow works
- Chaos: the plan goes off the rails immediately; you have to adapt
- Mature: the plan determines who lives, who dies, and who gets away
- Outcomes: Act 3 branch selected, possible secret unlock

### Beat 19 — The Confrontation
- 3-card sequence with escalating stakes
- Location by route: courthouse/precinct, barbershop/bar, warehouse/dock, safe house/private meeting
- 1980s reference: courthouse has payphones; barbershop has a Zenith; warehouse has a forklift
- 1990s reference: precinct has a vending machine; bar has a jukebox; warehouse has a boombox
- 2000s reference: courthouse has a fax machine; bar has an iPod; warehouse has a laptop
- Present-day reference: courthouse has security cameras; bar has a sound system; warehouse has smart locks
- Humor: the confrontation is interrupted by something absurd; a cookout, a block party, a church bake sale
- Chaos: multiple NPCs show up uninvited; sides shift mid-scene
- Mature: violence, betrayal, and redemption all possible; no option is clean
- Outcomes: evidence, confrontation result, final trust shifts

### Beat 20 — The Reckoning
- Ending resolution based on final state
- 1980s reference: ending montage scored by Public Enemy; newspaper clippings on a wall
- 1990s reference: ending montage scored by Wu-Tang; Polaroids on a corkboard
- 2000s reference: ending montage scored by Kanye; video slideshow on a laptop
- Present-day reference: ending montage scored by Kendrick; TikTok-style recap with text overlays
- Humor: even the death ending has a punchline; the block still jokes about you
- Chaos: secret ending requires collecting all receipts; the final reveal is absurd but earned
- Mature: endings reflect cumulative choice; no one is purely innocent
- Outcomes: canonical ending or secret ending; epilogue cards show NPC fates
